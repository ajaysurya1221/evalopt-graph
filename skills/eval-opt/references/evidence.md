# Evidence and research

For each conclusion that affects acceptance, keep a compact evidence record:
claim; located source or artifact; observation method; candidate or source
revision; freshness; status; and material limits. Useful statuses are observed,
inferred, unverified, contradicted, stale, and superseded. They describe evidence
in this workflow; they are not kernel decision codes.

Match the source to the claim. Command output establishes what a command observed
under its conditions. Source inspection establishes properties supported by the
inspected code. User instructions establish the requested contract. Official
documentation establishes what its publisher documents. None alone establishes
every behavior, runtime, deployment, or general safety claim.

An agent-written success file, self-assigned trust tier, or worker summary is not
independent check provenance. Inspect underlying artifacts and their relationship
to the actual candidate. A controller-owned observation can support a claim only
within that controller's verified trust boundary. Hashes identify bytes; they do
not authenticate authorship or make the contents true.

When researching, define the questions and required coverage before searching.
Prefer primary material and current sources for changing facts. Inspect the
source behind a snippet and look for relevant contradictions or counterexamples.
Keep disagreements visible until evidence resolves them. A missing external
source is a limitation, not permission to fabricate a citation. Local-only
research should identify that scope.

Spend the remaining research budget on questions that could change the answer.
Stop when coverage is adequate, new rounds provide no useful information, or
the bound is reached. State uncertainty for unresolved central claims. Do not
turn a plausible inference into an observed fact to satisfy a completion gate.

Retain failed checks and material redactions. Do not copy credentials, private
unrelated content, or full unnecessary traces into reports. Keep the exact
reproduction procedure, salient observations, and located evidence sufficient
to understand both the result and its limits.
